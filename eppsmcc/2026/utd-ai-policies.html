The Policy Landscape

Three documents, three audiences

Document Issued by Governs
UTDSP5017 Policy Office (2025-03-24) Generative AI in academic work
Initial Guidelines for Gen AI Office of Information Technology Everyday best practice, campus-wide
AI Usage Guidelines Information Security Office + OIT Faculty/staff official duties

They are complementary, not redundant. Academic conduct is governed by UTDSP5017; employment duties and data handling are governed by the ISO guidelines.

Anchoring policies

Generative AI does not sit in a policy vacuum. It inherits from:

  • UTDSP5003 — Student Code of Conduct
  • UTDBP3096 — Information Security and Acceptable Use
  • UTDSP5010 — FERPA Policy
  • HIPAA, copyright and licensing law
  • TRAIGA — Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (signed June 22, 2025)

UTDSP5017

Generative AI Use in Academic Work

Policy statement. UT Dallas recognizes the evolving landscape of technology and its integration into academic life. Generative AI creates new content from patterns learned in existing data.

  • Scope: coursework, independent research, dissertations, theses
  • Grounded in the Student Code of Conduct (UTDSP5003)
  • Goal: a framework preserving academic and personal integrity while encouraging creativity and innovation

The faculty prerogative

Central design choice

Faculty have the freedom and responsibility to determine how generative AI may be used in academic work under their supervision.

  • Faculty may permit, prohibit, or partially allow generative AI
  • Expectations must be communicated before academic work begins for the semester
  • Guidance belongs in the syllabus and in assignment descriptions
  • When permitting, faculty should specify the citation style

Three regimes

Permitted

Use ethically and responsibly. Document and attribute per APA/MLA/Chicago or the professional style the instructor names. Validate all output.

Partial

Allowed for some work, not all. Follow the written guidelines; faculty detail permitted uses in the assignment prompt. Attribution still required.

Prohibited

Unauthorized use is a violation of UTDSP5003 — covering ideating, outlining, writing, studying, and generating text, tables, code, analysis, video, or images.

What the policy asks of students

  • AI output does not replace your understanding and interpretation of the course material
  • But: workforce-ready students may need generative AI skills, appropriate to their discipline
  • Because AI is now embedded in ordinary software, students should get written guidance on which specific applications are off-limits
  • Verification of AI output is the student’s responsibility, not the tool’s

Academic integrity clause

Applies to students, faculty, and staff alike:

  • No use for activity that is illegal or fraudulent, or that violates state/federal law or UT System policy
  • Compliance with Information Security and Acceptable Use, and with FERPA
  • Misuse producing academic dishonesty — or jeopardizing protected university or student data — is subject to discipline under the Student Code of Conduct

OIT Initial Guidelines

Five practical rules

  1. Protect confidential data
  2. Review content before publication
  3. Adhere to existing academic policy
  4. Be alert for phishing
  5. Connect with OIT before procuring AI tools

1. Protect confidential data

  • Do not enter data classified as confidential into publicly available generative AI tools
  • Information shared under default settings is not private and may expose proprietary or sensitive material to unauthorized parties
  • No FERPA or HIPAA information may be used in generative AI tools

2. Review before publication

AI-generated content can be:

  • inaccurate
  • misleading
  • entirely fabricated (“hallucinations”)
  • encumbered by copyrighted material

You are responsible for verifying any content containing AI-generated material.

3–5. Policy, phishing, procurement

  • Policy: review the Undergraduate Catalog policies, Graduate policies, and the Acceptable Use Policy
  • Phishing: generative AI lets malicious actors produce sophisticated phishing and deepfakes at scale — keep reporting suspicious messages
  • Procurement: OIT has already licensed tools with appropriate privacy and security protections; contact OIT before buying your own

ISO AI Usage Guidelines

Scope and definition

“AI” here means both content-generating algorithms (art, video, music, text from prompts) and software agents that perform work on behalf of a faculty or staff member.

  • Primary audience: faculty and staff performing official duties
  • Useful to all campus members
  • Explicitly not intended to govern academic conduct, plagiarism, or student academic settings — see UTDSP5017 for that
  • Published by ISO in partnership with OIT; reviewed periodically

Three named risks

  • Security of University Data may be undermined when processed by AI tools
  • Security of UT Dallas Information Systems may be undermined when AI agent software is installed
  • Output is not guaranteed accurate — models carry bias, inaccuracy, and copyright exposure, and may learn from imperfect internet sources

Approved tools first

Default posture

Use AI tools provided by UT Dallas rather than personal access to public or vended platforms, which have not been evaluated for security.

Currently offered by OIT:

  • CometAI
  • Microsoft Copilot
  • Amazon Bedrock

Third-party hosted solutions with AI features must be reviewed by the Office of Contracts Administration (OCA).

Acceptable use of other tools

  • Public Data may be entered into generative AI tools
  • AI may support research not classified as containing Confidential Data
  • Instructors may use AI to develop instructional materials — provided no Confidential Data is uploaded to non-UTD tools
  • Faculty and staff are accountable for deliberate and unintentional misuse, for disseminating AI misinformation, and for exposing Confidential Data
  • Faculty and staff are accountable for actions performed by AI agents on their behalf

Unacceptable use

  • Personally subscribed AI tools may not be used in the performance of job duties and may not receive Confidential Data (per UTDBP3096)
  • Watch your login context — it is easy to work with University Data inside a personal subscription by accident
  • Public tools should not generate output that would count as official University Data — e.g., non-public research, academic work, instructional materials

Regulated data and contracts

Regulations

  • AI use in job duties cannot violate state or federal law, UT System policy, or UT Dallas policy
  • The University Attorney approves scenarios where regulated data may be used
  • Never place HIPAA or FERPA data into personally obtained AI tools

Contracts and licensing

  • Adhere to the licensing terms of the tool and of any dataset accessed through it
  • Some data is contractually barred from AI input regardless of UTD approval

Additional considerations

  • Automation — weigh impact on employees, applicable law (incl. TRAIGA), and the risk of outcomes a human would not produce
  • Audit logging — prefer tools with transaction logs; agents acting in a user’s context are especially hard to audit, and risky agent software may be removed
  • Training — prefer contracted training content; faculty and staff must complete assigned AI training
  • End of contract — specify acceptable data destruction methods
  • Oversight — impactful AI decisions should be reviewed and approved by a knowledgeable human

Ethical use

  • Human rights and privacy — no discriminatory, harassing, or defamatory content
  • Bias and discrimination — models trained on incomplete or biased data can output the same; avoid perpetuating bias by race, gender, age, or other protected characteristics
  • Transparency and accountability — be transparent about AI-generated content; you are responsible for anything you present as your own original work
  • Harm — no content harming individuals, groups, communities, or the university
  • Constructive content — align output with the university’s values and mission

Reporting and consultation

  • Questions, comments, concerns: infosecurity@utdallas.edu
  • If your work cannot fit within the recommended boundaries, request a consultation rather than improvising
  • OIT can advise on safe handling of University Data and Information Systems

Putting It Together

Who does what

Students

  • Read the syllabus; assume nothing. Ask for the rule in writing.
  • Cite AI use in the required style. Verify every claim.

Faculty

  • State the AI regime before the semester’s work begins, in syllabus and assignments.
  • Keep Confidential Data out of non-UTD tools when preparing materials.

Staff

  • Use UTD-provided tools for job duties. Watch your login context.
  • Route new AI procurement through OIT and OCA.

Decision rules

Four questions before you prompt

  1. Is this academic work? → UTDSP5017 governs; the instructor’s rule controls.
  2. Is this a job duty? → Use a UTD-provided tool (CometAI, Copilot, Bedrock).
  3. What is the data classification? → Public data only in public tools. Never FERPA or HIPAA.
  4. Would I stake my name on the output? → You already have. Verify before publishing.

Themes across all three documents

  • Human accountability is non-transferable — the user owns the output, and owns what an agent does on their behalf
  • Data classification is the operative distinction, not tool preference
  • Disclosure and citation are the default, not the exception
  • Verification is mandatory — hallucination is treated as an expected failure mode
  • Governance is provisional — all three documents anticipate revision as tools and law evolve
