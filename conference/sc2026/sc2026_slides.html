Modeling Media Effects in Taiwan Elections: Latent Voter Segments, Bayesian Networks, and Synthetic Data



Karl Ho

University of Texas at Dallas

John Marion Glendenning

University of Texas at Dallas

Prepared for presentation at the Conference on Electoral Politics in Taiwan hosted at the University of South Carolina Columbia, SC, August 9-10, 2026.

The Question

We report that partisan media moves vote share by 2–4 points.

That number comes from a system with a liberal–conservative dimension, two parties, and unusually stable identities.

Is it a fact about media, or a fact about American political structure?

Taiwan holds the method constant and varies the context: a media system aligned on national identity, a third force taking 4% to 27%, and three cycles spanning a wide range of cross-Strait threat.

Three Elections

Year Result Context
2016 Tsai 56.1 / Chu 31.0 / Soong 12.8 Post-Sunflower; KMT weakened
2020 Tsai 57.1 / Han 38.6 / Soong 4.3 Hong Kong shock — Tsai trailed by 25 pts in early 2019
2024 Lai 40.1 / Hou 33.5 / Ko 26.5 Threat normalized; third force brackets China

Data: TEDS presidential waves. Method follows Ho and Glendenning (2026) on the US case.

What Had to Change

Every estimation component transferred unchanged.

. . .

The graph did not.

. . .

Following Achen and Wang (2017), national identity and cross-Strait position sit on the causal spine — not as controls, but as the organizing variable.

. . .

Ethnicity drops out; identity absorbs its role.

Does the Synthetic Data Hold Up?

Marginals and joint cells track the observed data.

. . .

Adversarial AUC near chance — here the diagonal is the target.

. . .

Coefficients overlap.

. . .

Two caveats: AUC sits modestly above the US figures, and NoVote is over-predicted by ~3 points in every wave.

The Central Result

Change in DPP vote share under three interventions.

Common scale — panel width is directly comparable.

. . .

Media effects are real and larger than the US benchmark.

. . .

They are also dwarfed by the China factor.

The Magnitudes

Intervention 2016 2020 2024
Media exposure 4.4 7.5 9.2
Cross-Strait position 31.6 41.1 31.2
National identity 53.5 58.3 55.2

US benchmark for media: 2–4 points. Taiwan runs roughly double — and growing.

Our outlet coding attenuates toward zero, so these are lower bounds.

Is It the Hong Kong Effect?

China-factor effects do peak in 2020, as the account predicts.

. . .

But the ratio declines: 12.10 → 7.78 → 5.99.

. . .

Not because the China factor weakens.

Because media effects grow faster. The denominator doubles.

. . .

Dominance is eroding as the party system fragments.

The Cleavage the Model Rests On

Taiwanese identification rises steeply in younger cohorts.

Chinese identification is residual — never above 10% in any cell.

. . .

The gradient is why identity enters as a node rather than a control.

. . .

It is also why five age bands are needed where the US paper used four.

Does Pan-Blue Media Ever Backfire?

Kao (2026) found experimental backfire among PRC-skeptics.

. . .

We find mostly reinforcement: all cells positive in 2016 and 2024.

. . .

One negative cell — 2020, dual identifiers, −5.9.

. . .

Suggestive, not established. Wrong subgroup, one wave, one cell of nine.

What Synthetic Data Is For

Counterfactuals with no factual counterpart. No experiment assigns national identity. Graph mutilation supplies the quantity the Achen–Wang debate is actually about.

Comparison across incommensurable instruments. TEDS and ANES ask different questions in different languages. The estimands are identical.

But: it inherits source bias, can fail in ways that look like findings, and chance-level AUC says nothing about whether the graph is right.

Conclusion

Media effects have no universal magnitude. 4.4 to 9.2 points here against 2–4 in the US.

The dominant cleavage sets a ceiling. Media operates at the margin of a question most Taiwanese voters have already settled.

That ceiling is rising. The China factor’s relative dominance halved across three cycles.

The framework transfers; the graph does not — and having to respecify it is a feature.

Additional Material

Slides below are held for discussion

Why Not Silicon Samples?

Argyle et al. (2023): condition an LLM on a demographic backstory. Tetrachoric correlations above 0.9.

Bisbee et al. (2024): compressed variance, 48% of coefficients differ significantly, prompt sensitivity, temporal instability, demand effects.

Santurkar et al. (2023): LLMs reflect liberal, educated, Western opinion — poor footing for a cleavage discussed in Mandarin.

The deeper problem: none of these is diagnosable from inside the method.

One Quantity, Two Approaches

\[P(\text{DPP vote} \mid \text{Age} = 20\text{–}29, \text{Media} = \text{Green})\]

Silicon sample: write a prompt, sample, tabulate. Traceable to nothing. May differ next month.

Bayesian network: read from a conditional probability table fitted to TEDS respondents in that cell. Inspectable, checkable, stateable.

Why Latent Class Analysis First

Vote choice conditioning on all upstream variables:

Specification Configurations Free parameters
No latent segment 6,480 19,440
Latent segment, k = 6 324 972

Wave n is in the low thousands. Twenty-fold reduction.

Also absorbs unobserved heterogeneity, and segments are interpretable.

Outlet Alignment Moves

Huang (2009) codes EBC and Era as pan-Blue for 2008.

2020 rankings place both, plus Next TV, on the pan-Green side.

. . .

Two substantial outlets reverse. No single time-invariant coding is right for all three waves.

. . .

We withhold partisan classification from every contested outlet.

The Error Runs the Safe Way

Coding a partisan outlet as neutral is measurement error in the treatment.

It attenuates the estimated effect toward zero.

. . .

Our claim is that Taiwanese media effects are large.

The measurement choice works against our own conclusion.

A Null With a Methodological Cause

LCA recovered a near-deterministic non-voter class in each wave.

Every other class sits at 95–100% turnout.

. . .

Lines are flat because the responsive classes are at ceiling.

. . .

We report neither confirmation nor disconfirmation of the Zaller mechanism.

A failure of our specification, not a null result.

DAG Sensitivity

Dropping the direct identity and cross-Strait edges to vote choice.

. . .

We expected the media effect to inflate — all China-factor influence forced through the media path.

. . .

It shrinks instead: 4.4→3.8, 7.5→5.6, 9.2→8.1.

The media effect is not an artifact of the fuller specification.

Turnout: Reported vs Actual

Wave Self-reported Official Overreport
2016 ~86% 66.3% ~20 pp
2020 ~88% 74.9% ~13 pp
2024 ~88% 71.9% ~16 pp

Present in the raw files — not introduced by our harmonization.

Compresses the mobilization estimand against a ceiling. We treat turnout effects as the least reliable quantity in the analysis.

Limitations

  • Mobilization estimates unreliable — self-report ceiling plus 3-point NoVote over-prediction
  • HTE could not be estimated — LCA partitioned on turnout, not engagement
  • 2016 media coefficients anomalous — both regimes reduce DPP share, unexplained
  • Small cells — Chinese identifiers under 4% per wave; 2024 logistic shows quasi-complete separation
  • Graph correctness — identity ↔︎ media is plausibly bidirectional

Future Research

  • Recover the engagement gradient — re-estimate LCA excluding turnout from the indicators
  • Date the alignment shifts — content analysis between 2008 and 2020; CTi licence non-renewal as an anchor
  • Exploit the panel — dynamic Bayesian networks for identity feedback
  • Extend backward — 2008 and 2012 waves to test whether media growth is a trend
  • Disaggregate platform — LINE, PTT, Dcard have no US analogue

The Validation Nobody Has Done

Kao (2026) gives a randomized estimate of pro-Beijing media effects in the 2020 election.

Align the estimand — restrict to the China Times audience, match the outcome measure.

Compare an observational do-calculus estimate against an experimental one in the same election.

To our knowledge no synthetic-data causal estimate has been validated this way.
