Taiwanese phone polls face 90% refusal rates. TPOC now sells silicon-sample polling at NT$100 per question — benchmarked at 4.56 pp average error against real phone surveys.

The alternative to survey research is no longer hypothetical.

The Warning That Decides This Paper

TPOC’s own research director reports the models stereotype by age:

Group Model’s preset
35–54 most steadfast, calm, responsible
Highly educated rational verifiers
Elderly conservative, hesitant

A study of generational cleavage cannot use a method that already holds beliefs about generations.

A Bayesian network holds none. Cohort probabilities are estimated; where thin, the smoothing is a stated prior with a known weight.

A Cleavage That Is Closing

Taiwanese identity has moved one direction for three decades.

Over 60 in 2024: roughly split between exclusively Taiwanese and dual identity

Under 30: overwhelmingly Taiwanese

Usually read as a story about where Taiwan is heading.

We ask a different question — about how political influence works once a cleavage closes.

The Argument

Achen and Wang (2017): Taiwanese voting is organized by national identity.

That is a claim about the electorate in aggregate.

But a variable can organize an electorate while losing its power to discriminate within a subgroup.

If nearly all young voters identify as Taiwanese, identity tells you little about how one young voter differs from another.

Hypothesis: media fills that space — mattering more, not less, for the cohort whose identity is most settled.

Reach and effect are different quantities.

Three Elections

Year Result Generational note

2024 is the puzzle: the cohort with the most Taiwan-centric identity declined to vote for the party that owns that position.

Design

\[\Delta_a(m) = P\!\left(Y \mid do(M = m),\, A = a\right) - P\!\left(Y \mid A = a\right)\]

Change in outcome within age band \(a\), measured against that band’s own baseline.

  • Latent class segmentation, then a year-specific Bayesian network
  • Identity → cross-Strait position on the causal spine
  • Synthetic microdata at 5× observed size
  • Interventions by graph mutilation — delete edges into the node, then sample

That last step separates \(do(M=m)\) from conditioning — necessary, because Taiwanese voters select media consistent with identity.

Findings

Media Effects by Cohort

Monotonic in every wave. Rank correlation with age: −0.9, −0.8, −0.9.

. . .

Ages 20–29: 10.2 → 15.4 → 22.6

Ages 60+: 3.0 → 6.4 → 6.8

. . .

The gap is widening.

What Media Competes Against

Identity-to-media ratio, ages 60+:

22.9 → 10.6 → 10.8

. . .

Ages 20–29:

4.8 → 3.1 → 0.99

. . .

In 2024 the two interventions move the youngest cohort by the same amount.

The Number That Matters

2024, voters aged 20–29:

Intervention Effect on DPP share
National identity 22.3 pp
Media exposure 22.6 pp
Cross-Strait position 16.0 pp

Television news moves these voters as much as national identity does.

Eight years earlier the ratio was 4.8 to 1.

Is It Really Homogeneity?

Identity entropy rises with age: 0.51 → 0.82

Correlates at 0.78 with the identity/media ratio.

. . .

Media entropy is flat — 0.95 to 1.00 everywhere.

. . .

Young cohorts are homogeneous on identity specifically, not in general.

That is the discriminating evidence.

The Cohort We Cannot Predict

Unpredictable on Both Outcomes

Vote choice: full-model AUC 0.68–0.76 under 40, against 0.90–0.93 over 60.

Two-thirds of that gap survives adding identity, cross-Strait position, and media together.

Turnout: self-reported, inflated 13–20 points, compressed against a ceiling.

Heterogeneous effects could not be estimated at all — the latent solution partitioned on turnout, not engagement.

The cohort that matters most to the argument is the one we predict least well — on both quantities.

Why: Identity Stops Discriminating

China factor adds 0.154 for the young, 0.251 for the old.

. . .

Media adds ~0.03 — flat across all cohorts.

. . .

The gap narrows because identity does less, not because media does more.

. . .

Nothing we have replaces it.

What the Synthetic Data Is Actually For

Synthetic data
Marginals, joint distributions reproduced almost exactly (TVD 0.001–0.016)
Individual prediction (AUC) no contribution

Not a tension. The same finding from two directions.

A variance-reduction device for distributional quantities — not an information-generation device for individual ones.

Augmentation gains were largest for the largest sample. We tested it against the wrong target.

Would Better Data Help?

Social Media: The Variance Argument Fails

Only 2020 has the platform battery. 2016 has none; 2024 has one generic item.

Media entropy among 20–29 is already 0.96–0.98. TV exposure is not low-variance for them.

LINE and YouTube adoption is near-universal — a use-indicator would be near-constant in exactly the cohort needing discrimination.

Same failure mode as identity.

Scraped Data Is Worse, Not Better

PTT posters, YouTube commenters, Facebook sharers are not samples of users.

They are the vocal minority — most engaged, most partisan, most willing to broadcast.

A tail-weighted measure for a median-voter prediction problem.

莊伯仲 warned of exactly this at the TPOC symposium: 同溫層偏誤.

And no platform carries age. Linkage runs through geography — back to the ecological problem.

Aggregate Data Will Not Rescue Turnout

CEC publishes registered voters by age. That is the denominator.

Turnout by age: we could not confirm it is published.

So age-specific turnout is an ecological inference problem, not a retrieval one.

And EI is badly suited to age here: young-heavy districts are Taipei and Hsinchu; old-heavy districts are Yunlin and Pingtung.

Age composition is confounded with partisan geography.

Where This Points

Every difficulty is about recovering individual-level information on young voters.

None obstructs aggregate estimation — and aggregates are what the synthetic data already reproduces well.

Change the estimand. DPP share within an age group, not the vote of a given respondent.

For forecasting and allocation that is the quantity actually wanted. It does not license claims about individual behaviour.

Conclusion

A closing cleavage does not just become less contested. It loses its power to discriminate where closure is most complete.

Media effects among the young double: 10.2 → 22.6

Identity effects among the young more than halve: 48.7 → 22.3

Three qualifications: turnout shows no gradient; the mechanism weakens in 2024 (rho 0.6); the predictive payoff is small.

Three cross-sections give a snapshot, not a film. Cohort, period, and life cycle are not separable here.

Silicon Samples Are Already Commercial

TPOC 台灣議題研究中心, August 2026:

  • 3,000 AI agents, 28 attributes, 26 years of polling data
  • Benchmarked against Meilidao and TVBS phone polls
  • Average error: 4.56 percentage points

Three Warnings From That Room

Variance compression. NTU’s 馮勃翰: AI answers cluster in middle options. Bisbee, replicated in Taiwan.

Echo-chamber bias. 莊伯仲: feeding agents social media produces 同溫層偏誤.

Ecological fallacy. UT Austin’s 林澤民: AI agents are prone to it.

Bayesian calibration. 謝邦昌: hybrid human-agent designs will require it.

Turnout Is Not Vote Choice

Rank correlation of media effect with age:

Vote choice: −0.9, −0.8, −0.9

Turnout: +0.2, +0.4, +0.1

Turnout effects are small — 1.5 to 6.0 points against 3.0 to 22.6.

Self-reported turnout runs far above official. We read this as uninformative, not null.

Does Synthetic Augmentation Help?

It does not.

Mean gain: 40–59 = +0.026, under 40 = +0.011, 60+ = +0.006.

. . .

The largest gain goes to the largest sample — backwards from the sparsity story.

. . .

Two of three under-40 gains are ≈ 0.

The Methodological Point

Zaller predicts heterogeneity in media effects by political awareness.

This suggests a complementary axis: heterogeneity by how much room the organizing variable has left to move within a subgroup.

Effect heterogeneity should be estimated with respect to the distribution of the dominant cleavage — not assumed uniform.

The Measurement Caveat

Our media variable is television-only — what TEDS supports consistently across all three waves.

This understates young-voter exposure specifically. LINE, PTT, and Dcard carry political content the instrument does not capture.

The rising “None” category among the young means absence of television news, not absence of political information.

This works against the hypothesis. If young voters get partisan content through unobserved channels and we still recover larger television effects, the true asymmetry is larger.

Media Diet by Cohort

Why Bayesian Networks, Not Silicon Samples

Argyle et al. (2023): condition an LLM on a demographic backstory. Tetrachoric correlations above 0.9.

Bisbee et al. (2024): compressed variance, 48% of coefficients differ significantly, prompt sensitivity, temporal instability, demand effects.

Santurkar et al. (2023): LLMs reflect liberal, educated, Western opinion.

The deeper problem: none of those failures is diagnosable from inside the method.

One Quantity, Two Approaches

\[P(\text{DPP vote} \mid \text{Age} = 20\text{–}29, \text{Media} = \text{Green})\]

Silicon sample: write a prompt, sample, tabulate. Traceable to nothing.

Bayesian network: read from a conditional probability table fitted to TEDS respondents in that cell.

Inspectable. Cell count checkable. Provenance stateable.

Why Latent Class Analysis First

Specification Configurations Free parameters
No latent segment 6,480 19,440
Latent segment, k = 6 324 972

Wave n is in the low thousands. Twenty-fold reduction.

It does not create information. It imposes a constraint — all demographic variation acts on vote choice through the segment.

Media Coding: Alignment Moves

Huang (2009) codes Eastern Television and Era as pan-Blue for 2008.

2020 audience rankings place both on the pan-Green side.

No single time-invariant coding is right for all three waves.

We withhold partisan classification from every contested outlet — which attenuates the estimated effect toward zero.

The error runs against our own conclusion.

Cohort Versus Period

Three cross-sections cannot separate the two.

Is the pattern fixed at socialization, or do individuals become less media-responsive as they age?

TEDS panel components would support a dynamic specification. This is the first item in future research.

Future Research

  • Platform-specific measurement — LINE, PTT, Dcard for the waves where available
  • Cohort versus period — dynamic Bayesian networks on the panel component
  • The third-force channel — model “Other” as substantive, not residual
  • Extend backward — 2008 and 2012 waves to test whether the pattern tracks the progressive closure of the cleavage
